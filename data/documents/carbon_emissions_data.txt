GLOBAL CARBON EMISSIONS DATA SUMMARY
Source: Global Carbon Project, IEA, IPCC AR6 (2021-2023)

=============================================================================
SECTION 1: GLOBAL CO2 EMISSIONS OVERVIEW
=============================================================================

Global CO2 emissions from fossil fuels and industry reached approximately 36.8 billion
tonnes (Gt CO2) in 2022, a record high. This represents an increase of 1% from the
previous record of 36.3 Gt CO2 in 2019, prior to the COVID-19 pandemic.

Year-by-year milestones:
  - 1990: 22.7 Gt CO2
  - 2000: 25.4 Gt CO2
  - 2010: 33.1 Gt CO2
  - 2019: 36.3 Gt CO2 (pre-pandemic record)
  - 2020: 34.0 Gt CO2 (COVID-19 pandemic decline of 5.4%)
  - 2021: 36.3 Gt CO2 (post-pandemic recovery)
  - 2022: 36.8 Gt CO2 (new record)

Land-use change (deforestation, agriculture) adds approximately 3.9 Gt CO2 per year,
bringing total anthropogenic CO2 emissions to approximately 40.7 Gt CO2 in 2022.

SECTION 2: EMISSIONS BY COUNTRY (2022)
=============================================================================

China is the world's largest CO2 emitter, accounting for approximately 30% of global
fossil fuel CO2 emissions:

  1. China:         11.5 Gt CO2   (31.2% of global total)
  2. United States:  4.9 Gt CO2   (13.3%)
  3. India:          2.8 Gt CO2   (7.6%)
  4. Russia:         1.9 Gt CO2   (5.2%)
  5. Japan:          1.1 Gt CO2   (2.9%)
  6. Germany:        0.7 Gt CO2   (1.8%)
  7. South Korea:    0.6 Gt CO2   (1.6%)
  8. Canada:         0.6 Gt CO2   (1.5%)
  9. Iran:           0.6 Gt CO2   (1.6%)
 10. Saudi Arabia:   0.6 Gt CO2   (1.6%)

Top 10 emitters collectively account for approximately 68% of global CO2 emissions.

Cumulative historical emissions (1850-2022):
  - United States: ~410 Gt CO2 (largest historical contributor)
  - European Union: ~370 Gt CO2
  - China: ~280 Gt CO2

Global greenhouse gas emissions by sector (CO2-equivalent):

  Energy (electricity & heat):  34%

  Industry (manufacturing):     24%
  Agriculture:                   11%
  Buildings:                      6%

  - Oil combustion:   33% of energy CO2
  - Natural gas:      27% of energy CO2

=============================================================================
SECTION 4: GREENHOUSE GAS COMPOSITION
=============================================================================

Carbon dioxide (CO2) is the dominant greenhouse gas but not the only one:

  CO2 (fossil fuels & industry):  65% of total GHG warming contribution
  CO2 (land-use change):           11%
  Methane (CH4):                   16%
  Nitrous oxide (N2O):              6%
  Fluorinated gases (F-gases):      2%

Methane (CH4):
  - Global warming potential (GWP100): 27.9x CO2
  - Global warming potential (GWP20):  81.2x CO2
  - Major sources: natural gas/oil leaks (fossil fuels), livestock enteric
    fermentation, rice paddies, landfills, wetlands
  - Global CH4 emissions: approximately 580 million tonnes/year

Nitrous oxide (N2O):
  - Global warming potential (GWP100): 273x CO2
  - Major sources: synthetic fertilizers, livestock manure, soil disturbance

SECTION 5: RENEWABLE ENERGY AND EMISSIONS
=============================================================================

Electricity sector CO2 emissions declined by approximately 1.3% in 2022 in
OECD countries despite strong economic growth, due to growth in solar and
wind generation.

Renewable energy share of global electricity generation (2022):
  - Total renewables:  29.9%
  - Hydropower:        15.3%
  - Wind:               7.6%
  - Solar PV:           4.9%
  - Bioenergy:          2.4%
  - Other renewables:   0.4%
  - Nuclear (low-carbon, non-renewable): 9.2%

CO2 intensity of electricity generation (gCO2/kWh, 2022 global average):
  - Coal:            820 g CO2/kWh
  - Natural gas:     490 g CO2/kWh
  - Nuclear:           12 g CO2/kWh lifecycle
  - Solar PV:          38 g CO2/kWh lifecycle
  - Onshore wind:      11 g CO2/kWh lifecycle
  - Hydropower:         4 g CO2/kWh lifecycle
  - Global average:   436 g CO2/kWh

Decarbonizing electricity to <50 gCO2/kWh by 2035 (IEA NZE scenario) requires
adding approximately 1,020 GW/year of solar and wind capacity — 3x the current
annual deployment rate of ~340 GW/year.

=============================================================================
SECTION 6: CARBON BUDGET AND TARGETS
=============================================================================

The Paris Agreement (2015) established two temperature targets:
  - Well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels
  - Best efforts to limit to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels

Remaining carbon budget (as of January 2023, IPCC AR6):
  - 50% probability of staying below 1.5°C:  ~500 Gt CO2
  - 67% probability of staying below 1.5°C:  ~380 Gt CO2
  - 50% probability of staying below 2.0°C:  ~1,500 Gt CO2

At current emission rates of ~40 Gt CO2/year, the 1.5°C carbon budget
will be exhausted in approximately 9-12 years (by ~2032-2035).

Net-zero CO2 targets by major emitters:
  - European Union: 2050
  - United States:  2050
  - China:          2060
  - India:          2070

=============================================================================
SECTION 7: CARBON CAPTURE AND STORAGE (CCS)
=============================================================================

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology captures CO2 emissions from power
plants and industrial facilities before they enter the atmosphere. The captured
gas is compressed and injected into underground geological formations — typically
depleted oil and gas reservoirs or saline aquifers — for long-term storage.

Current CCS deployment (2022):
  - Global operational CCS capacity:  ~45 million tonnes CO2/year
  - Required by 2050 (IEA NZE scenario): ~7,600 million tonnes CO2/year

CCS remains expensive at $50-100/tonne CO2 for power plants and is not yet
deployed at the scale required for climate targets. The IPCC identifies CCS as
an important but not sufficient mitigation option.
